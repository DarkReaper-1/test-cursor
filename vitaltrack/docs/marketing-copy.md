# VitalTrack — Marketing copy

Every line below is checked against the app's actual behavior. Nothing
here says or implies camera/fingerprint blood pressure measurement.

## Tagline options
- "Heart and blood pressure tracking that tells you the truth."
- "Track what your phone can measure. Log what it can't."
- "No camera tricks. Just honest heart and blood pressure tracking."

## One-liner (for a landing page hero)
VitalTrack measures your heart rate with your camera, and helps you track
blood pressure from the monitor you already own — clearly, honestly, with
no account required.

## Landing page sections

**Section: "What VitalTrack actually does"**
> Your iPhone's camera can measure your heart rate. It cannot measure
> your blood pressure — no phone can, no matter what another app's
> screenshots suggest. VitalTrack tells you which is which, every time.

**Section: How heart rate measurement works**
> Cover your camera and flash with your fingertip. Hold still for about
> 20 seconds. VitalTrack reads the tiny brightness changes caused by
> blood flow with every heartbeat — the same principle behind a pulse
> oximeter — and shows you a live signal-quality indicator so you always
> know how much to trust the number.

**Section: How blood pressure tracking works**
> Take your reading the way you always have — with your own monitor.
> Type it in, or let VitalTrack pull it automatically from a
> Bluetooth-enabled cuff, your Apple Health history, or a CSV file. Every
> reading, one place, one set of trends.

**Section: Built to be trusted**
> No account required. No ads. No selling your health data — there's
> nowhere to sell it to, because VitalTrack doesn't have a server. Your
> data lives on your device, and in your own iCloud or Apple Health only
> if you choose to turn that on.

**Section: Subscriptions without the tricks**
> Free means free: unlimited logging, heart rate measurement, 30 days of
> history, CSV export. Premium is $4.99/month or $39.99/year, shown to
> you before you're ever asked to pay — no hidden trial, no surprise
> charge, cancel anytime from your Apple ID settings.

## App Store review–response guidance

When a review claims the app "doesn't measure blood pressure" as a
complaint, the standard response should not apologize for the limitation
— it should point back to onboarding/FAQ and reiterate that no phone can
do this, while acknowledging where the *in-app clarity itself* could be
improved if that's the actual complaint. Never respond by suggesting the
feature "is coming soon."
