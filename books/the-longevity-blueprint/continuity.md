## 1. Book-Wide Conventions

**Evidence tiers** (used consistently in every chapter, introduced fully in Chapter 4):
- **Well-established** — broad scientific consensus, supported by multiple high-quality sources (systematic reviews, major guideline bodies).
- **Promising, less certain** — supportive evidence exists but is preliminary, mixed, or based on limited trial data.
- **Preliminary or unproven** — early-stage, mechanistic, or anecdotal; explicitly flagged as not yet ready to act on with confidence.

**Chapter structure template** (every chapter follows this shape):
1. Engaging introduction / hook
2. The science, explained simply, with evidence tiers labeled
3. Real-world examples / scenarios
4. Practical actions
5. "Action Plan" — concise, numbered
6. Key takeaways (bulleted, 3–6 items)

**Language guardrails (non-negotiable, checked every chapter):**
- Never claim a lifestyle change "cures," "reverses," or "eliminates" a diagnosed disease. Approved phrasing: "can reduce risk of," "is associated with improvement in," "may help support."
- Never instruct readers to start, stop, or adjust a prescription medication.
- Never provide individualized diagnostic statements ("you have..." / "this means you are...").
- Every disease-specific chapter (Part III especially) includes at least one explicit "talk to your doctor" checkpoint.
- No invented statistics, study names, quotations, or guideline numbers. Use `[VERIFY: source type]` inline flags for anything that would normally need a citation; log the flag here.

**Recurring frameworks/terms established so far (definitions must stay consistent everywhere they recur):**
- *Healthspan* vs. *lifespan*
- *Hallmarks of aging* (biological aging processes)
- *Metabolic syndrome* as shared root of obesity/diabetes/hypertension/dyslipidemia
- *Evidence tiers* (see above)
- *The four-system energy model*: sleep, nutrition, movement, stress (Part IV spine)
- *Habit engine* / behavior-change mechanism (cue–routine–reward, implementation intentions, environment design, habit stacking) — introduced in Ch. 5, referenced (not re-explained) in every later Action Plan

**Recurring reader tools to keep consistent across chapters:**
- Self-assessment worksheet style (first used in Ch. 13's Blueprint synthesis, reused in Ch. 20 and Ch. 27)
- Weekly checklist format (introduced Ch. 27, should echo earlier chapters' Action Plans in format)
- 12-week planning framework (introduced Ch. 20, mirrors the structure of the previously drafted "Reverse Your Health" sample chapter)

## 4. Style/Voice Notes

- Second person ("you"), encouraging, non-judgmental. Avoid fear-based framing ("if you don't do this, you'll...") in favor of benefit-forward framing ("people who do this tend to...").
- Avoid absolute claims ("always," "never," "guaranteed") outside of the medical-safety guardrails themselves (e.g., "never adjust medication without your doctor" is an appropriate absolute).
- Keep jargon minimal; define any technical term the first time it's used per chapter (even if defined earlier in the book) since readers may not read linearly.
